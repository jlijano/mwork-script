# MWORK Agent Training Guide

This guide explains how to teach the MWORK call framework so agents understand the logic instead of sounding like they are reading a script.

---

# 1. Training Objective

An agent should be able to:

- explain why MWORK is calling,
- establish rapport quickly,
- identify the customer's current claim status,
- determine who is paying,
- confirm the decision-maker,
- identify whether the vehicle is driveable and safe,
- explain MWORK's independent-shop positioning,
- match customer needs to relevant benefits,
- handle objections without arguing,
- close to a specific next action,
- and document the call accurately.

The goal is **conversation competence**, not memorization.

---

# 2. Teach the Framework First

Before teaching exact wording, agents should memorize the ten-stage model:

**CONNECT → CARE → DISCOVER → QUALIFY → SAFETY → POSITION → SOLVE → HANDLE → CLOSE → DOCUMENT**

If an agent understands this flow, they can recover even when the customer interrupts or the conversation takes an unexpected direction.

---

# 3. What Agents Should Memorize

Agents should memorize only a small number of high-value anchors:

- the opening purpose,
- the MWORK independent-repair positioning,
- the core vehicle-safety questions,
- the approved insurance/compliance statements,
- the main close types,
- and the objection-handling framework.

Everything else should be learned as concepts and choices.

---

# 4. What Agents Should NOT Memorize Word-for-Word

Avoid forcing agents to memorize:

- long feature lists,
- every objection response verbatim,
- every insurance explanation,
- every benefit in one pitch,
- or long closing paragraphs.

Word-for-word memorization often produces:

- robotic delivery,
- poor listening,
- failure to adapt,
- awkward repetition,
- and missed customer cues.

---

# 5. Opening Training

The opening should accomplish four things quickly:

1. identify the correct customer,
2. identify the agent/MWORK,
3. reference the vehicle,
4. provide a practical reason for the call.

Agents should practice multiple natural versions while preserving the approved meaning.

### Coaching checkpoint

Ask the trainee:

> “If the customer interrupts you after ten seconds, will they already understand who you are and why you called?”

If not, the opener is too long.

---

# 6. Empathy Training

Empathy is not a required sentence.

It is a behavior.

Teach agents to listen for:

- injury,
- stress,
- family concerns,
- transportation problems,
- frustration with insurance,
- urgency,
- confusion,
- and fear of cost.

The response should fit what the customer actually said.

### Bad

“I’m sorry to hear that” after the customer says everyone is okay.

### Better

“I’m glad everyone is okay.”

### Coaching exercise

Give trainees ten different customer statements and require them to respond naturally without using the same empathy phrase twice.

---

# 7. Discovery Training

Teach agents to gather information in this order:

1. vehicle,
2. claim,
3. transportation,
4. repair facility,
5. documentation.

Agents should practice **not asking questions they already know the answer to**.

### Example

If the customer says:

“My car was towed to ABC Collision and State Farm already gave me a claim number, but I don’t have a rental yet.”

A weak agent asks:

“Where is your car? Have you filed a claim? Do you have a rental?”

A strong agent says:

“Got it — so the vehicle is already at ABC Collision and State Farm has the claim. The main thing that hasn’t been arranged yet is the rental, correct?”

That shows active listening.

---

# 8. Qualification Training

Agents must know:

- who the decision-maker is,
- who the payor is,
- whether another repair facility has been selected,
- and whether repair work has already begun.

Teach agents that these questions protect everyone's time.

An agent should not deliver a full pitch before learning whether the customer can actually make the repair decision.

---

# 9. Driveability Training

The vehicle-safety section should be treated as operationally serious.

Agents should understand why each question matters, not just memorize it.

### Airbags
May indicate significant impact and safety-system involvement.

### Broken glass/mirrors
May affect safe visibility or expose occupants.

### Lights
Damaged headlights/taillights can make the vehicle unsafe or unlawful to operate.

### Fluid leak
May indicate mechanical or safety risk.

### Wheels/tires
May create immediate road-safety risk.

### New dash warning lights
May indicate damaged vehicle systems.

### Pulling
Could indicate suspension/alignment/structural damage.

### Hood/doors/trunk
Improper closure can create road or occupant safety concerns.

Agents are not mechanics and should not diagnose damage. Their job is to identify potential safety concerns and route the vehicle appropriately.

---

# 10. Positioning Training

Agents should be able to explain MWORK in plain language.

The source's central positioning is:

- MWORK is independent,
- MWORK works with insurance companies,
- MWORK handles estimates/payments and repair coordination,
- and the customer retains repair-facility choice.

Agents should be trained to explain this in short sections instead of one long speech.

---

# 11. Benefit-Matching Training

Create role-play cards with a customer problem on one side and possible MWORK benefits on the other.

Examples:

### Customer problem
“I don’t have another car.”

### Relevant benefits
Rental coordination / transportation support.

---

### Customer problem
“I’m worried about repair quality.”

### Relevant benefits
Certifications, warranty, manufacturer capability, full-service repair.

---

### Customer problem
“I don’t want this to be complicated.”

### Relevant benefits
Insurance coordination, paperwork support, towing/transport, in-house capabilities.

---

### Customer problem
“I don’t know your company.”

### Relevant benefits
Locations, years in business, warranty, certifications.

### Coaching standard
The trainee should select **the smallest number of benefits needed** to answer the concern.

---

# 12. Objection Training

Teach agents to classify objections before responding.

Common categories:

- cost,
- trust,
- insurer authority,
- convenience,
- transportation,
- switching friction,
- uncertainty,
- perceived commitment,
- and soft rejection.

Then use:

**Acknowledge → Clarify → Answer → Evidence → Question**

### Training exercise

Manager gives the objection:

> “I already have an estimate.”

Agent must first identify the likely concern:

> “The customer may think an estimate means they are already committed.”

Only then should they respond.

This trains thinking rather than memorization.

---

# 13. Closing Training

Agents should practice closing toward specific outcomes.

## Inspection close
“Let’s get the inspection scheduled.”

## Tow close
“Let’s confirm where the vehicle is so we can discuss the pickup process.”

## Rental close
“Let’s verify what has already been arranged for your rental.”

## Document close
“If you send the estimate/claim information, we can review what the next step should be.”

## Follow-up close
“What time would be better for me to call you back?”

## Respectful decline
“Understood. I’ll note that you’ve chosen another direction.”

### Coaching standard
A call should rarely end with only:

> “Call us if you need anything.”

That is not an operational next step.

---

# 14. Voicemail Training

The source voicemail emphasizes:

- customer name,
- agent name,
- year/make/model,
- inspection/rental help,
- phone number,
- repeat phone number,
- warm close.

The source explicitly says to repeat the phone number more slowly the second time.

That is good practice and should remain part of voicemail coaching.

### Voicemail rules

- Keep it short.
- State the practical reason for calling.
- Do not overload with benefits.
- Repeat the callback number clearly.
- Sound calm and helpful.

---

# 15. Call-Control Training

Conversation leadership does not mean interrupting or pressuring the customer.

It means:

- acknowledging what they said,
- summarizing their current situation,
- asking the next logical question,
- and preventing the call from becoming disorganized.

### Example

Customer gives a long story involving towing, insurance, and another shop.

Agent response:

“Got it. So your vehicle is at the tow yard, the claim is already filed, and insurance hasn’t scheduled the inspection yet. Let’s start with the vehicle so we can figure out the next step.”

That is strong call control.

---

# 16. Compliance Training

Agents must know the difference between:

- approved facts,
- general talking points,
- estimates,
- program eligibility,
- and absolute promises.

Potentially sensitive categories in the source include:

- no-money-out-of-pocket statements,
- repair-choice/legal-right language,
- OEM-only statements,
- rental coverage,
- insurer payment obligations,
- deductible assistance,
- total-loss outcomes,
- liability timing,
- inspection timing,
- and warranty scope.

Only approved wording should be treated as mandatory language.

When unsure, agents should escalate rather than improvise.

---

# 17. Suggested Training Progression

## Phase 1 — Knowledge
Learn the call architecture and MWORK positioning.

## Phase 2 — Guided practice
Use the live call flow with notes visible.

## Phase 3 — Branching role-play
Customer changes direction mid-call.

## Phase 4 — Objection drills
Agent must diagnose the concern before answering.

## Phase 5 — Safety scenarios
Driveable vs non-driveable decision practice.

## Phase 6 — Closing drills
Agent must secure a specific next step.

## Phase 7 — Full mock calls
End-to-end calls with CRM documentation.

## Phase 8 — QA review
Score recorded/mock calls and coach gaps.

---

# 18. Certification Standard for New Agents

Before handling live leads, an agent should demonstrate that they can:

- complete the opening naturally,
- show appropriate empathy,
- identify the decision-maker,
- determine the payor path,
- perform driveability screening correctly,
- explain MWORK clearly,
- match benefits to needs,
- handle at least five core objections,
- close to a next action,
- and complete CRM notes accurately.

---

# 19. Coaching Philosophy

Do not coach only on exact words.

Coach on:

- missed information,
- weak listening,
- unnecessary feature dumping,
- failure to identify the objection,
- unsupported promises,
- poor transitions,
- missed closes,
- and incomplete documentation.

The best agent is not the one who sounds most like the written script.

The best agent is the one who can consistently navigate the customer's situation while preserving the script's approved intent.
