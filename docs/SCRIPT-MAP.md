# MWORK Script Map

This document maps the major content blocks found in the source script into their operational purpose.

The original source is dense because multiple document types are combined in one place. The cleanest way to understand it is by function.

---

# 1. Insurance / Coverage Notes

The source begins with insurance-path information such as:

- insured using own policy,
- claimant using the other driver's policy,
- deductible references,
- uninsured-motorist references,
- liability acceptance/establishment,
- coverage verification,
- crash-report collection,
- recorded statements.

## Purpose
These notes help the agent understand that not every accident lead is in the same insurance situation.

## Operational use
This belongs in a knowledge base or claim-routing guide, not in the main live-call script.

---

# 2. Voicemail

The source contains a voicemail model that includes:

- customer name,
- agent name,
- vehicle year/make/model,
- inspection/rental reason,
- callback number,
- repeated callback number,
- warm closing.

## Purpose
Give the customer enough context to recognize the call as accident/vehicle related and call back.

## Key coaching note
The source explicitly advises repeating the phone number more slowly the second time and ending warmly/confidently.

---

# 3. Initial Outreach Call

The source tells the agent to:

- ask for the customer,
- reference the vehicle,
- explain the practical reason for calling,
- ask how they are doing,
- listen for immediate needs,
- use short personal conversation when appropriate.

## Purpose
Establish relevance and trust before moving into qualification.

---

# 4. Rapport / Conversation Leadership

The source uses concepts like:

- express empathy,
- genuine concern,
- check whether the customer can talk,
- briefly identify and connect with them,
- respond appropriately to personal information.

## Purpose
Prevent the call from feeling like an insensitive accident-sales pitch.

---

# 5. Decision-Maker Check

The source asks whether the person is:

- the one discussing repairs,
- handling the claim,
- working with insurance,
- deciding where the vehicle will be repaired.

## Purpose
Confirm authority before investing in a full pitch.

---

# 6. Mandatory Disclosures / Core Positioning

The source introduces MWORK as:

- a full-service repair facility,
- independent,
- working directly with insurance companies,
- able to assist with estimates and payments,
- and positioned around customer choice of repair facility.

## Purpose
Explain the business model and differentiate MWORK from insurer-directed repair pathways.

---

# 7. Payment-Path Qualification

The source asks whether the repair is:

- out of pocket,
- through the customer's own insurance,
- or through someone else's policy.

## Purpose
Route the customer into the correct claim and cost conversation.

# 9. Driveability / Safety Section

The source asks whether the vehicle is driveable and safe and includes questions involving:

- airbags,
- glass,
- mirrors,
- lights,
- leaks,
- wheels/tires,
- warning lights,
- exhaust,
- pulling,
- hood,
- trunk/liftgate,

## Purpose

## Main branches

# 10. Process Explanation

The source includes different process messaging for non-driveable and driveable vehicles.

For non-driveable vehicles it references pickup, insurance inspection, parts ordering/repair, and potential total-loss handling.

For driveable vehicles it points toward estimate/inspection and transportation planning.

## Purpose
Help the customer understand what happens next.

---

# 11. Directional / Opening Questions

The source includes questions about:

- vehicle location,
- claim filing,
- insurance contact,
- rental,
- other transportation,
- number of calls received,
- car seats,
- crash report,
- prior accident experience.

## Purpose
Discover the customer's current state and identify gaps MWORK may be able to solve.

---

# 12. Early Interruptions

The source anticipates questions such as:

- Who told you to call me?
- Are you with my insurance?
- Did insurance tell you to call?
- How did you get my information?
- Who are you?
- Why are you calling?

It also references crash-report/public-record sourcing in its response language.

## Purpose
Handle suspicion immediately and transparently enough to continue the conversation.

---

# 13. Closing Questions

The source contains a closing section heading but provides less structured material here than in the discovery and objection sections.

## Purpose
This should be the conversion point of the call.

## Gap
The close needs more explicit next-action choices such as inspection, tow, rental, document exchange, callback, or disposition.

---

# 14. Objection Table

The source explicitly organizes objections into:

- objection,
- what it really means,
- response,
- close,
- return to pitch / ask question.

Covered objections include insurer recommendations, speed, repair guarantees, existing estimates, simplicity, cost, waiting for insurance, vehicle already at another shop, unfamiliarity with MWORK, insurer preference, rental concerns, switching friction, thinking it over, and potential out-of-pocket cost.

## Purpose
Teach agents to solve the underlying concern rather than react to the words alone.

---

# 15. Independent-Body-Shop Positioning

The source contains a long argument in favor of independent repair facilities, emphasizing:

- customer focus,
- fewer insurer contractual constraints,
- repair quality,
- manufacturer standards,
- transparency,
- OEM-parts positioning,
- customer advocacy.

## Purpose
Provide the philosophical foundation of the MWORK sales position.

## Best use
Use selectively and positively. Do not turn the call into a broad attack on every DRP or insurer-recommended facility.

# 20. Predict-Future Language

The source includes guidance for what the customer may hear from insurance after choosing an independent repair facility and what the customer can say in response.

## Purpose
Prepare the customer for future insurance conversations and reduce surprise.

## Risk
Predictive or absolute statements about insurer behavior should be reviewed carefully and should not be presented as universally guaranteed outcomes.

---

# 21. Sales-Technique Headings

The source references:

- FEEL FELT FOUND,
- IF I COULD WOULD YOU,
- INTRO,
- OPENING QUESTIONS,
- BUILD TRUST W/CREDIBILITY.

These appear to be training reminders rather than complete script sections.

## Purpose
Signal the intended sales methodology.

## Improvement
Translate these into modern, explicit behaviors:

- Acknowledge → Normalize → Explain
- Conditional close tied to a real customer obstacle
- Structured discovery
- Proof matched to doubt

---

# Final Source Architecture

The original script can therefore be classified into these major systems:

1. **Lead Contact System**
2. **Empathy / Rapport System**
3. **Decision-Maker Qualification System**
4. **Insurance Routing System**
5. **Vehicle Safety System**
6. **Repair-Facility Positioning System**
7. **Benefit / Credibility System**
8. **Objection-Handling System**
9. **Closing System**
10. **Operational / CRM System**
11. **Agent Training System**
12. **Knowledge Base / Reference System**

That is why the source feels much larger than a normal call script: it is effectively an entire sales-and-service playbook stored in one sheet-like document.
