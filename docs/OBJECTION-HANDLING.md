# MWORK Objection-Handling Master Guide

The MWORK source script does something unusually valuable: it does not only list customer objections. It attempts to identify **what the objection really means**.

That is the correct way to train objection handling.

The agent's job is not to defeat the customer in an argument. The agent's job is to understand the concern, answer it clearly, and return the conversation to the next useful step.

---

# Core Framework: ACAEQ

Use this sequence:

## A — Acknowledge
Show that you heard the customer.

## C — Clarify
Ask a short question if needed to identify the real concern.

## A — Answer
Give the relevant information using approved language.

## E — Evidence
Use the most relevant MWORK proof point only if needed.

## Q — Question
Return to discovery or the next action.

The final question prevents the conversation from dying after a rebuttal.

---

# 1. “MY INSURANCE TOLD ME TO GO TO THEIR SHOP”

## What it usually means
The customer trusts the insurer and assumes the recommendation is a requirement.

## Agent objective
Clarify whether the customer has actually committed the vehicle or merely received a recommendation.

## Response logic
- Acknowledge that insurer recommendations are common.
- Explain MWORK's approved repair-choice language.
- Avoid attacking the recommended shop.
- Ask whether the vehicle has already been delivered or whether only an appointment/estimate exists.

## Bridge question
“Is the vehicle already at that facility, or did they just give you the shop information?”

---

# 2. “THEY SAID IT WILL BE FASTER IF I USE THEIR SHOP”

## What it usually means
The customer prioritizes speed and convenience.

## Agent objective
Find out what “faster” means to the customer: estimate, rental, repair start, repair completion, or claim approval.

## Response logic
- Acknowledge that speed matters.
- Clarify which part of the process they are concerned about.
- Explain MWORK's relevant operational capability.
- Do not promise a faster repair unless the timeline is actually known.

## Bridge question
“When you say faster, is your biggest concern getting the inspection done, getting into a rental, or getting the repair completed?”

---

# 3. “THEY SAID THEY CAN’T GUARANTEE THE REPAIR IF I DON’T USE THEIR SHOP”

## What it usually means
The customer fears losing claim protection or repair support.

## Agent objective
Separate insurer coverage/claim handling from the repair facility's workmanship responsibility, using only approved legal/compliance wording.

## Response logic
- Acknowledge the concern.
- Clarify exactly what the insurer said.
- Explain MWORK's warranty and insurer-coordination process where applicable.
- Avoid making categorical legal statements unless approved.

## Bridge question
“Did they say they would deny the claim, or were they talking specifically about who guarantees the repair work?”

---

# 4. “I ALREADY HAVE AN ESTIMATE FROM THEIR SHOP”

## What it usually means
The customer thinks an estimate equals a final repair commitment.

## Agent objective
Determine whether repair authorization or work has actually started.

## Response logic
- Acknowledge that they already completed an important step.
- Determine whether the vehicle is still movable and whether repairs have begun.
- Explain the next available options using approved process language.

## Bridge question
“Has any repair work been authorized or started yet, or do you only have the estimate at this point?”

---

# 5. “I JUST WANT TO GO THROUGH INSURANCE AND KEEP IT SIMPLE”

## What it usually means
The customer wants less effort and fewer decisions.

## Agent objective
Position MWORK's insurer coordination as a simplicity benefit.

## Response logic
- Agree with the desire to keep things simple.
- Explain that MWORK works with insurance on estimates and payments.
- Focus on reducing administrative burden.

## Bridge question
“What part is still not set up for you right now — the inspection, the rental, or the repair facility?”

---

# 6. “I DON’T WANT TO PAY ANYTHING OUT OF POCKET”

## What it usually means
Fear of unexpected cost.

## Agent objective
Identify whether the concern is deductible, uncovered repair cost, rate difference, parts, storage, or another charge.

## Response logic
- Do not immediately promise zero out-of-pocket cost.
- Clarify the concern.
- Explain approved pricing, deductible, insurance, or assistance-program information.
- Distinguish insurance coverage from MWORK programs.

## Bridge question
“Are you mainly concerned about your deductible, or did the carrier specifically tell you there could be additional repair charges?”

---

# 7. “I’LL JUST WAIT AND SEE WHAT INSURANCE SAYS”

## What it usually means
Uncertainty, avoidance, or a belief that the insurer controls the next step.

## Agent objective
Find out whether there is actually anything scheduled.

## Response logic
- Acknowledge that waiting for guidance is understandable.
- Clarify whether an adjuster is assigned.
- Determine whether inspection, rental, towing, or repair selection is already arranged.

## Bridge question
“Have they already assigned an adjuster and scheduled the vehicle inspection?”

---

# 8. “I’VE ALREADY DROPPED THE CAR OFF AT THEIR SHOP”

## What it usually means
The customer feels committed or believes switching would be difficult.

## Agent objective
Determine the actual stage of work.

## Response logic
- Ask whether repairs have been authorized or started.
- Clarify whether the shop is only storing/estimating the vehicle.
- Explain approved transfer/transport options if appropriate.

## Bridge question
“Have they started repairs yet, or is the vehicle there waiting for the estimate or insurance approval?”

---

# 9. “I’VE NEVER HEARD OF YOUR SHOP”

## What it usually means
Trust barrier.

## Agent objective
Build credibility without dumping every company credential.

## Useful proof categories
- locations,
- years in business,
- warranty,
- certifications,
- manufacturer credentials,
- repair capability,
- relevant specialization.

## Bridge question
“That’s fair. When you choose a repair facility, what matters most to you — certification, warranty, location, repair quality, or how they work with insurance?”

This allows the customer to tell the agent which proof matters.

---

# 10. “I JUST WANT TO DO WHATEVER INSURANCE RECOMMENDS”

## What it usually means
The customer prefers to delegate the decision.

## Agent objective
Educate without becoming confrontational toward the insurer.

## Response logic
- Acknowledge that following the insurer's recommendation feels easier.
- Explain that MWORK can also coordinate with insurance.
- Present customer choice as information, not pressure.

## Bridge question
“Has the insurer already arranged everything for you, including the repair appointment and rental?”

---

# 11. “IS THIS GOING TO DELAY MY RENTAL CAR?”

## What it usually means
Transportation is the customer's priority.

## Agent objective
Determine the actual rental status and coverage.

## Response logic
- Avoid unconditional coverage promises.
- Ask whether rental coverage or third-party rental authorization has already been confirmed.
- Explain how MWORK can help coordinate where applicable.

## Bridge question
“Has the carrier already approved or reserved a rental for you?”

---

# 12. “I DON’T WANT TO DEAL WITH SWITCHING SHOPS”

## What it usually means
Friction avoidance.

## Agent objective
Determine whether there is actually a complicated transfer or merely a perceived one.

## Response logic
- Acknowledge that moving the vehicle sounds inconvenient.
- Clarify the current location and repair status.
- Explain approved coordination/transport support.

## Bridge question
“Where is the vehicle right now, and has any repair work actually started?”

---

# 13. “I’LL THINK ABOUT IT”

## What it usually means
Could be a soft no, unresolved concern, lack of urgency, or desire to end the call.

## Agent objective
Respect the response while identifying whether a specific question remains unanswered.

## Response logic
- Do not pressure.
- Ask one diagnostic question.
- If the customer still wants time, schedule a follow-up only if they agree.

## Bridge question
“Of course. Before I let you go, is there one thing you’re still unsure about — cost, insurance, repair quality, or timing?”

If they say no, close respectfully.

---

# 14. “MY INSURANCE SAID THEY WON’T PAY / I MIGHT HAVE MONEY OUT OF POCKET IF I USE YOUR SHOP”

## What it usually means
High fear of unexpected cost and insurer pressure.

## Agent objective
Get the exact carrier statement rather than rebutting a summary.

## Response logic
- Acknowledge the concern.
- Ask exactly what the carrier said would not be paid.
- Determine whether this relates to deductible, labor rate, parts, procedures, storage, betterment, or another item.
- Use approved MWORK pricing/claim-coordination language.
- Escalate when needed instead of guessing.

## Bridge question
“Did they tell you which specific charge they believed you would be responsible for?”

# Early-Call Trust Objections

## “WHO ARE YOU?”
Answer directly: identify yourself and MWORK, then state the reason for the call.

## “WHY ARE YOU CALLING?”
Tie the call to the customer's vehicle and potential help with inspection, repair, towing, or rental.

## “ARE YOU WITH MY INSURANCE COMPANY?”
Answer directly that MWORK is an independent repair facility, then explain that MWORK works with insurance companies on estimates/payments.

## “HOW DID YOU GET MY INFORMATION?”
Use only MWORK-approved disclosure language regarding the data source. The source script references crash-report/public-record information; legal/compliance approval should govern the final wording.

# Objection Handling Rules

1. **Never argue.**
2. **Never insult the insurer or another shop.**
3. **Never invent policy coverage.**
4. **Never turn a general training note into an absolute promise.**
5. **Clarify before rebutting.**
6. **Use one relevant proof point, not the entire company pitch.**
7. **End with a question or next action.**
8. **Respect a clear decline.**

---

# Modernizing FEEL–FELT–FOUND

The source references FEEL–FELT–FOUND.

Instead of forcing agents to use the literal formula, teach:

**Acknowledge → Normalize → Explain**

Example:

“I understand why that would be concerning. A lot of customers have questions when the insurer recommends a specific shop. What we can do is explain how MWORK works with the carrier and what your options are, then you can decide what makes sense for you.”

---

# Using “IF I COULD, WOULD YOU?”

Use this only after the real obstacle is known.

Formula:

**If we can solve [specific obstacle], would you be comfortable with [specific next action]?**

Examples:

- “If we can arrange the tow, would you be comfortable scheduling the inspection with us?”
- “If we can help coordinate the rental and inspection, would that solve the main issue you’re dealing with right now?”

Avoid vague or manipulative versions.

---

# Final Principle

The best objection response is often not a better rebuttal.

It is a **better question**.

When an agent understands the concern behind the objection, the conversation becomes consultative rather than adversarial.
